Who this is for

Deploying is frightening

because one person knows how and it involves a document. Deploys should be boring, repeatable, and something anyone on the team can run on a Friday.

The site got slow

as traffic or data grew. Worth measuring before spending: the fix is often an index and some caching rather than the larger instance you were about to buy.

The bill keeps climbing

and nobody is sure what for. Usually a handful of resources nobody turned off, and storage classes left at the default since the day the account was opened.

What the work actually is

  • 01
    Getting it deployed

    EC2, Nginx, Postgres, media on S3 and CloudFront in front. Certificates that renew themselves, and a deploy written down rather than remembered.

  • 02
    Backups you have tested

    An untested backup is a belief, not a backup. Restoring one is part of the job, not an optional extra to be done later.

  • 03
    Performance, measured

    Profiling first, then the fix, then the same measurement again. Database queries, caching, image delivery and asset pipeline, in the order the numbers justify.

  • 04
    Monitoring

    Enough to know something is wrong before a customer tells you, and few enough alerts that people still read them.

  • 05
    Cost

    Right-sizing, storage classes, and turning off what nobody uses. Frequently the cheapest week of work on this page.

What it costs

Performance work is usually the best-value item on this page, because it is bounded: measure, fix the top three, measure again. A fresh deployment is predictable. Inheriting infrastructure nobody documented is the one that needs a look before a number.

Defined work is quoted fixed. Where the current state is unknown, the first step is a short paid audit that produces the plan and the number, and the audit is yours regardless.

Questions I get asked

Should we move to Kubernetes?
Probably not. Most applications I see are well served by a couple of instances behind a load balancer, and the operational cost of a cluster is paid every week by whoever is on call.
Can you take over infrastructure someone else set up?
Yes. The first deliverable is a written description of what is actually running, which surprisingly often is the first time anyone has had one.
Will this need ongoing work?
Security patching does, yes. That can be a small retainer or it can be your team with a documented runbook — I would rather you had the choice than depended on me for it.
